### What this ranks

Which U.S. college athletic departments could be the largest and most motivated sponsorship-marketplace partners for Anvara. It ranks **opportunity**: the size of potential sponsorship dollars and reasons to pursue growth. It does **not** claim any school has unsold sponsorship inventory, and it is not a financial-distress tracker.

### Categories

| Category | Default weight | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Financial Pressure | 35% | How much incentive does the department have to grow its own revenue? |
| Commercial Opportunity | 45% | How large are the sponsorship dollars in play? |
| Growth Catalysts | 20% | Is there recent evidence of active commercial expansion? |
| Rights-holder intelligence | not scored | Who sells the school's sponsorships, and who to talk to |

These weights are **hypotheses, not validated predictors**. Adjust them in the sidebar.

### Definitions (FY2025, NCAA financial reporting categories)

- **Allocated revenue** = direct institutional support + indirect institutional support + student fees + direct government support.
- **Generated revenue** = total operating revenue − allocated revenue.
- **Reported surplus/deficit** = total operating revenue − total operating expenses, as reported (after subsidies). Scored as a **% of expenses** so large and small programs compare fairly; the dollar figure is always shown.
- **Self-sufficiency** = generated revenue ÷ total operating expenses.
- **Own-operations revenue** = generated revenue − (media rights + NCAA and conference distributions + post-season football revenue). "Generated revenue" is the NCAA's standard term but includes conference payouts the department doesn't control (often similar for every member) and donations. Own-operations revenue isolates what the department's own operations bring in: tickets, sponsorship and licensing, fundraising, concessions, camps and similar. Donations stay in, because fundraising is the department's own work.
- **Own-operations revenue vs. conference peers** = own-operations revenue ÷ median of the other FY2025 conference members with data. (A setting can switch this to NCAA generated revenue.) Not scored unless enough of the conference is covered (default 50%); a partial sample is never treated as the whole conference. Coverage is measured against **public** members, because private schools (e.g., Northwestern, USC, Vanderbilt, Duke, Notre Dame) don't publish NCAA financial reports. The benchmark is therefore a public-peer benchmark, and is labeled that way.
- **Commercial revenue** = the NCAA category combining corporate sponsorship, advertising, licensing and related items. **It is not pure sponsorship revenue.** Its share of total revenue is displayed and compared with peers but not scored, so schools aren't rewarded just for having low reported commercial revenue.
- **Fan reach** = 2024–25 average home attendance for football, men's basketball and women's basketball (default mix 60/25/15), each ranked across schools. Actual attendance only, never stadium capacity. Digital reach is shown when comparable figures exist but not scored.

Subsidies and student fees are subtracted once (in generated revenue) and never counted twice.

### Normalization

Each component is a **percentile rank among the schools analyzed**, rescaled so the lowest school scores 0 and the highest 100. Ranks can't be distorted by one outlier: a single program with far higher revenue moves only one position, not the whole scale. The trade-off is that ranks hide the size of gaps, so raw values are always shown next to scores. For Financial Pressure, *weaker* finances rank higher.

### Growth Catalyst rubric

Catalysts are rare events, so they use fixed points rather than ranks:

| Factor | Values |
|---|---|
| Significance | high 45 · medium 25 · low 10 points |
| Commercial relevance | direct ×1.0 · indirect ×0.5 |
| Confidence | confirmed ×1.0 · reported ×0.6 |
| Recency | ≤12 months ×1.0 · ≤24 months ×0.5 · older ×0.25 |

Several announcements about one underlying event share an event ID and count **once**. Totals are capped at 100. Institutional capital investment is a **positive** growth signal, not distress. NCAA-wide rule changes are market context, not points for every school; a school's specific response to them can score. Proposed legislation (e.g., the Protect College Sports Act) is a scenario, never treated as enacted law.

### Missing data

Missing data is **never scored as 0**.
- A category is scored from the components that have data, re-weighted, if they cover at least 60% of the category's weight; otherwise it shows as incomplete.
- The overall score needs categories covering at least 55% of total weight.
- For catalysts, "researched, none found" is a real 0; "not yet researched" is missing.
- **Confidence:** High = all components present (including all three sports in fan reach); Medium = all three categories, some components missing; Low = a category missing.
- **Data completeness** = share of ten key research inputs present.

### Data principles

- Primary public sources first: university NCAA financial reports, state transparency portals, official releases, NCAA statistics. EADA only as a cross-check (different accounting).
- Every figure stores its source, reporting year, retrieval date and verifier.
- New findings, including AI-assisted ones, enter a review queue and affect scores only after a person verifies the source.
- Contacts: public professional information only.
- Demonstration data is fictional, labeled on every screen, never mixed with research, and never exported.

### Limitations

- One fiscal year only (FY2025); multiyear trends are deliberately not scored.
- Rank-based scores show relative position within this set of schools, not absolute attractiveness.
- NCAA reporting categories are self-reported and vary in practice between schools.
- Weights reflect judgment and have not been validated against actual sponsorship outcomes.
